# Why most people quit their habits in week two

Everyone talks about motivation like it's the thing that makes habits stick.
It isn't. Motivation is highest on day one and lowest exactly when you need
it most: around day 10 to 14, when the novelty is gone and the results
haven't shown up yet.

The people who actually keep a habit going aren't more disciplined. They
just made the habit smaller than their motivation could ever drop. A habit
that only requires two minutes a day survives a bad week. A habit that
requires an hour a day does not.

There's a second reason habits die in week two: people attach their identity
to the outcome instead of the process. "I'm trying to get fit" collapses the
moment a workout gets skipped. "I'm someone who shows up" survives a skipped
workout, because showing up again the next day still counts as evidence.

Three things that actually move the needle:

1. Shrink the habit until missing a day feels almost impossible to justify.
2. Track the streak of showing up, not the streak of perfect performance.
3. Decide in advance what the minimum viable version looks like on a bad day.

None of this is exciting advice. It's also the only version of habit advice
that has ever survived contact with a genuinely bad week. If you've quit a
habit before, it probably wasn't a willpower problem. It was a design
problem.
